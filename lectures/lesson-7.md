## Progressive Neuro-Oncology Curriculum — Lesson 7

### Newly diagnosed grade 4 IDH-mutant astrocytoma: choosing between CATNON- and glioblastoma-style treatment

**Guidance checked November 7, 2026**

- [NCCN Central Nervous System Cancers, Version 3.2026 — August 17, 2026](https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/guidelines-detail?category=1&id=1425)
- [ASCO–SNO guideline for adult diffuse gliomas — December 2021; recommendations retained in the April 2025 rapid update](https://ascopubs.org/doi/full/10.1200/JCO.21.02036)
- [ASTRO WHO grade 4 adult-type diffuse-glioma guideline — September/October 2025](https://www.astro.org/provider-resources/guidelines/clinical-practice-guidelines/who-grade-4-adult-type-diffuse-glioma-gl)
- [Final CATNON analysis — *Lancet Oncology*, January 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41449147/)
- [cIMPACT-NOW Update 12 — published May 18, 2026; print September 2026](https://academic.oup.com/neuro-oncology/article/28/9/2069/8687120)

### Why this entity is different

**Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant, CNS WHO grade 4 is not glioblastoma.** The term “glioblastoma, IDH-mutant” is obsolete under WHO CNS5; glioblastoma is reserved for IDH-wildtype disease.

The diagnosis requires an IDH1 or IDH2 mutation, absence of whole-arm 1p/19q codeletion, and at least one grade 4 feature:

| Grade 4 feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Microvascular proliferation | Histologic grade 4 |
| Necrosis | Histologic grade 4 |
| CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion | Molecular grade 4, even without necrosis or microvascular proliferation |

ATRX loss and TP53 alteration commonly support astrocytic lineage. Confirm that the CDKN2A/B result represents **homozygous**, not hemizygous, deletion.

A molecularly defined grade 4 tumor may remain largely nonenhancing and resemble grade 2–3 disease radiographically. Conversely, a necrotic, strongly enhancing tumor may behave more like conventional high-grade disease. This biologic range is one reason no single postoperative regimen has been definitively established.

### Initial postoperative assessment

After maximal safe resection or biopsy:

- Obtain an early postoperative MRI, ideally within 24–48 hours.
- Document neurologic function, KPS, seizure burden and corticosteroid requirement.
- Review IDH1/2, 1p/19q, ATRX, TP53 and CDKN2A/B status.
- Consider broad sequencing because additional alterations may inform prognosis and trial eligibility.
- Obtain MGMT-promoter methylation, but do not use an unmethylated result alone to omit temozolomide; its predictive role is less clearly defined than in IDH-wildtype glioblastoma.
- Discuss clinical-trial eligibility before starting radiation.

### The central treatment decision

NCCN permits two principal strategies for a patient with good performance status. ASCO–SNO likewise states that grade 4 IDH-mutant astrocytoma may be treated either like grade 3 IDH-mutant astrocytoma or like IDH-wildtype glioblastoma because the evidence quality is very low.

| Strategy | Treatment | Evidence source |
|---|---|---|
| **Glioblastoma-style** | 60 Gy in 30 fractions with TMZ 75 mg/m² daily during RT, then TMZ 150–200 mg/m² on days 1–5 every 28 days for 6 cycles | Extrapolated from IDH-wildtype glioblastoma and supported for WHO grade 4 diffuse glioma by ASTRO |
| **CATNON-style** | 59.4 Gy in 33 fractions, followed by TMZ 150–200 mg/m² on days 1–5 every 28 days for 12 cycles; no concurrent TMZ required | Extrapolated from the IDH-mutant CATNON population and explicitly allowed by NCCN |

Both are guideline-concordant. There is no randomized trial directly comparing them in a pure WHO CNS5 grade 4 IDH-mutant population.

### How CATNON changes the discussion

The final CATNON analysis had 10.9 years of median follow-up. In IDH-mutant tumors:

- Twelve cycles of adjuvant TMZ improved median overall survival from **6.0 to 12.5 years**; HR 0.54.
- Concurrent TMZ did not produce a statistically significant overall-survival benefit.
- CDKN2A homozygous deletion and several other molecular alterations were prognostic, but none predicted lack of TMZ benefit.

CATNON originally enrolled grade 3, 1p/19q-noncodeleted tumors. Under current classification, a subset with CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion would now be grade 4. The final authors therefore argued that RT followed by 12 cycles of TMZ should also be considered for grade 4 IDH-mutant astrocytoma. This remains an extrapolation rather than a dedicated grade 4 randomized trial.

### A practical way to choose

| Clinical situation | Reasonable approach |
|---|---|
| Necrosis or microvascular proliferation, substantial enhancement, rapid growth or major residual disease | Many experts favor **60 Gy/30 with concurrent and adjuvant TMZ**, treating the phenotype like other grade 4 diffuse gliomas |
| Grade 4 assigned solely by CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion, without necrosis or microvascular proliferation | **59.4 Gy/33 followed by 12 cycles of TMZ** is particularly defensible using CATNON |
| Borderline pathology or important concern about concurrent-treatment toxicity | Sequential RT followed by TMZ avoids concurrent TMZ without abandoning the CATNON-proven component |
| Clinical trial available | Strongly consider the trial because neither standard approach is based on grade 4-specific randomized evidence |
| Older but reasonably fit patient | Hypofractionated RT, commonly 40.05 Gy in 15 fractions, with concurrent and adjuvant TMZ may be considered by extrapolation |
| Severe frailty or poor functional status | Individualize among 34 Gy/10 fractions, 25 Gy/5 fractions, TMZ alone in selected cases, or symptom-focused care |

Age alone should not determine intensity. Use functional status, cognition, comorbidities, tumor tempo, steroid requirement and the patient’s priorities.

### Tumor-treating fields

NCCN includes alternating electric fields as an option with adjuvant TMZ for supratentorial grade 4 IDH-mutant astrocytoma. However, the survival evidence comes from newly diagnosed glioblastoma trials dominated by IDH-wildtype tumors.

Therefore:

- **Guideline-supported option:** discuss tumor-treating fields after chemoradiation.
- **Uncertainty:** the magnitude of benefit specifically in IDH-mutant grade 4 astrocytoma is unknown.
- Review device burden, scalp toxicity, cost/access and the expectation of use for at least 18 hours daily.

### What not to substitute

Vorasidenib is approved for grade 2 IDH-mutant glioma after surgery. It is **not** an established replacement for RT plus alkylating chemotherapy in grade 4 disease.

Other IDH inhibitors, vaccines, PARP-inhibitor combinations and immunotherapy remain clinical-trial approaches in newly diagnosed grade 4 IDH-mutant astrocytoma.

### Surveillance and response assessment

NCCN follow-up for grade 4 IDH-mutant astrocytoma is:

- Brain MRI **2–8 weeks after completion of standard RT**
- Then every **2–4 months for three years**
- Then every **3–6 months indefinitely**

Under RANO 2.0, the post-RT MRI serves as the response-assessment baseline. During the first 12 weeks after RT, new or increased enhancement may represent pseudoprogression. If the patient is clinically stable, confirm suspected progression with short-interval MRI or pathology before changing therapy whenever appropriate.

Obtain earlier imaging for new neurologic deficits, increasing seizure frequency, cognitive decline or escalating corticosteroid requirements.

### Toxicity and supportive care

**Concurrent RT–TMZ**

- Monitor CBC regularly, commonly weekly.
- Provide PJP prophylaxis during the prolonged daily TMZ course and continue when substantial lymphopenia or corticosteroid exposure persists.
- Monitor nausea, constipation, fatigue, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, lymphopenia and hepatotoxicity.

**Adjuvant TMZ**

- Check CBC and hepatic/metabolic testing before each cycle.
- Dose-reduce or hold for cytopenias or significant nonhematologic toxicity.
- Do not automatically extend a six-cycle glioblastoma-style course to 12 cycles and call it evidence-based: six cycles derive from the Stupp approach, while 12 cycles derive from CATNON’s sequential regimen.

**General supportive care**

- Use dexamethasone only for symptomatic edema and taper to the lowest effective dose.
- Do not prescribe prophylactic antiseizure medication to a patient who has never seized.
- Discuss fertility preservation before RT and prolonged alkylating therapy when relevant.
- Address rehabilitation, cognition, mood, driving, work and caregiver burden early.

### At recurrence

There is no grade 4 IDH-mutant-specific salvage standard. Reconfirm true progression and consider:

- Clinical trial, preferred when feasible
- Repeat resection for a focal, safely accessible lesion or when tissue will change management
- Focused reirradiation for selected patients
- TMZ rechallenge, lomustine, bevacizumab for symptom/edema control, or another systemic option based on prior therapy
- Updated molecular profiling and an IDH-directed trial

Management should reflect the more indolent natural history some patients retain, rather than automatically applying every recurrent-glioblastoma assumption.

### Guideline versus expert practice and uncertainty

**Guideline-supported**

- Maximal safe resection followed by RT and TMZ
- Either concurrent/adjuvant TMZ or RT followed by adjuvant TMZ
- Tumor-treating fields as an option
- Clinical-trial consideration

**Expert practice**

- Favoring a Stupp-like regimen for histologically aggressive grade 4 disease
- Favoring a CATNON-like sequential regimen when grade 4 is assigned only by CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion
- These phenotype-based distinctions are rational but have not been tested prospectively.

**Uncertain**

- Whether concurrent TMZ adds benefit
- Whether six or 12 adjuvant cycles is optimal
- The benefit of tumor-treating fields
- How MGMT should modify treatment
- Whether histologic and molecular grade 4 tumors should receive identical therapy

**Emerging 2026 pathology update**

cIMPACT-NOW Update 12 concluded that **PDGFRA amplification is associated with grade 4-like behavior** in IDH-mutant astrocytoma and recommended considering it a grade 4 criterion. This is an important new consensus recommendation, but it is not yet a formal WHO CNS5 criterion and has not yet generated a distinct evidence-based treatment pathway. Other alterations—including CDK4/CDK6/CCND2 amplification, RB1 alteration, PIK3-pathway mutations and MYCN or EGFR alterations—were considered more consistent with intermediate, grade 3-like risk.

### What I should remember for clinic and boards

1. **IDH-mutant grade 4 astrocytoma is not called glioblastoma.**
2. Grade 4 requires necrosis, microvascular proliferation or **CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion**.
3. There is no dedicated randomized treatment trial for this WHO CNS5 entity.
4. NCCN allows both:
   - **Stupp-like:** 60 Gy/30 + concurrent TMZ → 6 adjuvant cycles
   - **CATNON-like:** 59.4 Gy/33 → 12 adjuvant cycles without concurrent TMZ
5. CATNON proved the benefit of the **adjuvant** phase; concurrent TMZ did not significantly improve OS.
6. Tumor-treating fields are an option, but the IDH-mutant-specific benefit is unknown.
7. Vorasidenib should not replace chemoradiation in grade 4 disease.
8. New in 2026: cIMPACT-NOW considers **PDGFRA amplification** consistent with grade 4 behavior, but this is not yet formal WHO CNS5 doctrine.
