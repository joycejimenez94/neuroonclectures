## Progressive Neuro-Oncology Curriculum — Lesson 1

### Adult diffuse gliomas: establishing the integrated diagnosis

**Guidance checked September 26, 2026.** The newest NCCN version I could verify is **Central Nervous System Cancers v2.2026, published June 10, 2026**. [CancerNetwork](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/nccn-expands-csf-molecular-profiling-recommendation-to-inoperable-high-grade-glioma?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 1. The diagnostic framework

Under WHO CNS5, adult-type diffuse gliomas fall into only three principal families:

| Integrated diagnosis           | Defining molecular feature                                            | Grades    |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| **Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant**    | IDH1/2 mutation; no whole-arm 1p/19q codeletion                       | 2, 3 or 4 |
| **Oligodendroglioma**          | IDH1/2 mutation **and** whole-arm 1p/19q codeletion                   | 2 or 3    |
| **Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype** | IDH-wildtype with qualifying histologic or molecular grade-4 features | 4         |

“Anaplastic astrocytoma,” “secondary glioblastoma” and molecularly defined “oligoastrocytoma” should generally not be used as final integrated diagnoses. Adult-type and pediatric-type diffuse gliomas are distinct families, even when pediatric-type tumors occur in adults. [academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/neuro-oncology/article/25/10/1731/7189810?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 2. Practical workup of a newly suspected diffuse glioma

**Guideline-based approach**

1. Obtain a high-quality MRI brain with and without contrast, including T2/FLAIR, diffusion and susceptibility sequences. Perfusion, spectroscopy or amino-acid PET can help with difficult differential diagnoses or biopsy targeting, but do not replace tissue.
2. Pursue **maximal safe resection** when feasible. The operative goal is maximal tumor removal while preserving neurologic function—not gross-total resection at the cost of a permanent deficit. If resection is unsafe, obtain a stereotactic biopsy with enough tissue for integrated molecular testing.
3. Obtain an early postoperative MRI, ideally **within 24–48 hours**, with pre/postcontrast imaging, DWI and T2/FLAIR to assess residual tumor and distinguish it from perioperative ischemia. [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41571-020-00447-z?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
4. Review the case in a multidisciplinary setting after the integrated diagnosis is available.

### 3. Minimum molecular checklist

A practical tissue algorithm is:

- **IDH1 R132H immunohistochemistry first.** If negative, sequence IDH1/IDH2 when the patient is younger, the morphology is not classic for glioblastoma, or the clinical setting remains suspicious for an IDH-mutant tumor.
- In an **IDH-mutant tumor**, evaluate ATRX and p53. Test for **whole-arm 1p/19q codeletion**, particularly when ATRX is retained or oligodendroglial morphology is present.
- In an **IDH-wildtype diffuse astrocytic tumor**, evaluate for:
  - Microvascular proliferation or necrosis
  - EGFR amplification
  - Combined chromosome 7 gain/chromosome 10 loss
  - TERT-promoter mutation
- In **IDH-mutant astrocytoma**, test for **CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion** because it establishes CNS WHO grade 4 even without necrosis or microvascular proliferation.
- Obtain **MGMT-promoter methylation** in glioblastoma/high-grade glioma. It predicts the likelihood of benefit from temozolomide but does **not** define the tumor type.
- Test H3 K27 alteration for midline tumors and H3 G34 mutation for appropriate hemispheric tumors, particularly in younger adults.
- Consider DNA-methylation profiling when histology and molecular findings conflict, tissue is limited, or the presentation is unusually young or atypical.

### 4. What the diagnosis changes immediately

| Final category                                       | Initial treatment direction                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Favorable **IDH-mutant grade 2** after surgery       | Observation, vorasidenib, or RT plus chemotherapy depending on residual disease, growth, symptoms and risk            |
| **Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant grade 3–4**                | RT plus alkylating chemotherapy, generally temozolomide-based                                                         |
| **Oligodendroglioma grade 3** or higher-risk grade 2 | RT followed by PCV is the evidence-based backbone; TMZ may be chosen when PCV toxicity is a major concern             |
| **Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype**, fit patient          | RT with concurrent and adjuvant temozolomide; tumor-treating fields may be discussed                                  |
| Older or frail patient with glioblastoma             | Individualize between hypofractionated RT, temozolomide, or combined therapy using performance status and MGMT status |

The **April 29, 2025 ASCO-SNO rapid update** conditionally recommends vorasidenib for postoperative IDH-mutant grade 2 astrocytoma or oligodendroglioma when RT and chemotherapy can safely be deferred. It is not a substitute for chemoradiation in a patient who needs immediate definitive treatment. [Journal of Clinical Oncology](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO-25-00250?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 5. Imaging follow-up

- **After surgery:** MRI within 24–48 hours.
- **After radiotherapy:** use the post-RT MRI as the response-assessment baseline.
- During the first **12 weeks after RT**, apparent worsening may represent pseudoprogression. If the patient is clinically stable and progression is not unequivocal, continue planned treatment and obtain confirmation imaging rather than immediately declaring failure. RANO 2.0 formally uses the post-RT MRI as baseline and recommends confirmation of suspected progression during this interval. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37774317/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- Typical NCCN surveillance intervals:
  - Grade 2: MRI every **3–6 months for 5 years**, then every **6–12 months**
  - Grade 3–4: approximately every **2–4 months for 3 years**, then every **3–6 months**

Shorten the interval for new symptoms, steroid escalation, increased seizures or equivocal radiographic change. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8099464/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 6. Supportive-care essentials

- Do not prescribe dexamethasone solely because a tumor is present. Use it for symptomatic edema or mass effect, at the **lowest effective dose**, and taper promptly.
- Do not routinely start an antiseizure medication in a patient who has never had a seizure. The SNO-EANO guideline finds insufficient evidence even for routine perioperative prophylaxis. [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/neuro-oncology/article/23/11/1835/6310125?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- After a tumor-related seizure, use an antiseizure medication; agents with fewer oncology drug interactions, such as levetiracetam or lacosamide, are commonly favored in practice.
- Anticipate steroid hyperglycemia, insomnia, mood effects, myopathy and infection risk.
- With temozolomide, monitor CBC, nausea, constipation and lymphopenia; assess PJP-prophylaxis needs during concurrent chemoradiation or prolonged corticosteroid exposure.
- With vorasidenib, monitor liver enzymes because transaminase elevation is an important toxicity.

### Guideline versus expert practice

- **Guideline:** Obtain tissue whenever safely possible and base management on an integrated histologic-molecular diagnosis.
- **Expert practice:** Broader sequencing or methylation profiling is often obtained early at major centers because it can clarify diagnosis and trial eligibility.
- **Uncertainty:** A TERT-promoter mutation alone in an otherwise low-grade-appearing IDH-wildtype lesion deserves careful clinicopathologic correlation; methylation profiling may prevent inappropriate assignment as glioblastoma.
- **Emerging/NCCN v2.2026:** When tissue sampling is not feasible in a suspected high-grade glioma, CSF tumor-derived DNA may be considered as an **adjunct**, especially for molecularly defined tumors such as H3 K27-altered diffuse midline glioma. It does not make tissue biopsy obsolete. [CancerNetwork](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/nccn-expands-csf-molecular-profiling-recommendation-to-inoperable-high-grade-glioma?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### What I should remember for clinic and boards

- **Oligodendroglioma = IDH mutation + whole-arm 1p/19q codeletion.**
- **CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion makes an IDH-mutant astrocytoma grade 4.**
- An IDH-wildtype diffuse astrocytic tumor can qualify as glioblastoma through molecular grade-4 features even without necrosis or microvascular proliferation.
- **MGMT is predictive, not diagnostic.**
- Do not sacrifice neurologic function for extent of resection.
- Postoperative MRI: **24–48 hours.**
- No routine antiseizure prophylaxis in a seizure-naïve patient.

## Progressive Neuro-Oncology Curriculum — Lesson 2

### Newly diagnosed glioblastoma: treatment of a fit adult

**Guidance checked October 3, 2026.** The newest NCCN CNS guideline I could publicly verify remains **Version 2.2026, published June 10, 2026**. This lesson also incorporates the **SNO–EANO glioblastoma consensus review published August 13, 2025** and the **ASTRO grade 4 diffuse-glioma guideline published September–October 2025**. [www.cancernetwork.com](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/nccn-expands-csf-molecular-profiling-recommendation-to-inoperable-high-grade-glioma?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Clinical starting point

This pathway applies to an adult with:

- Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype, CNS WHO grade 4
- Maximal safe resection or diagnostic biopsy completed
- Adequate performance status and organ function for combined treatment
- MGMT-promoter methylation status obtained, although treatment should not necessarily wait for the result

Before starting treatment, review the postoperative MRI, neurologic function, corticosteroid requirement, CBC/CMP and eligibility for a clinical trial.

### Standard postoperative treatment

For a fit adult, the standard backbone remains:

1. **Radiotherapy:** 60 Gy in 30 fractions over approximately six weeks.
2. **Concurrent temozolomide:** 75 mg/m² daily throughout radiation, including weekends.
3. **Adjuvant temozolomide:** after an approximately four-week break:
   - Cycle 1: 150 mg/m² daily on days 1–5 of a 28-day cycle
   - Subsequent cycles: increase to 200 mg/m² on days 1–5 if tolerated
   - Standard duration: six cycles
4. **Tumor-treating fields:** may be incorporated during maintenance temozolomide after discussion of expected benefit, device burden, scalp toxicity and patient preference.

The 2025 ASTRO guideline strongly supports concurrent RT–temozolomide followed by adjuvant temozolomide in eligible patients; alternating electric-field therapy is conditionally recommended. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40578479/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### How MGMT should affect the discussion

**MGMT-methylated:** Temozolomide benefit is greater, making the full combined regimen particularly compelling.

**MGMT-unmethylated:** Benefit from temozolomide is smaller, but for a younger or fit patient, MGMT status alone is generally insufficient to omit temozolomide outside a clinical trial. The evidence supporting MGMT-directed omission is strongest in older or frail patients, not in otherwise fit adults. [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/neuro-oncology/article/27/6/1412/8123008?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

A clinical trial replacing or intensifying standard treatment may be especially attractive for an MGMT-unmethylated tumor, but an investigational approach should not be presented as established superior therapy.

### Important decision points

| Clinical situation                                | Practical approach                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Good performance status after resection or biopsy | Six-week RT with concurrent TMZ, then adjuvant TMZ                                                                         |
| Slow postoperative recovery                       | Allow reasonable recovery, but avoid unnecessary treatment delay; RT commonly begins approximately 3–6 weeks after surgery |
| Significant residual enhancement                  | Does not change the chemoradiation backbone; consider trial eligibility                                                    |
| MGMT unmethylated                                 | Discuss smaller TMZ benefit and trials, but do not routinely omit TMZ in a fit younger patient                             |
| Unable to tolerate a six-week course              | Hypofractionated RT with or without TMZ may be reasonable                                                                  |
| Severe frailty or very poor performance status    | Individualized hypofractionated therapy or supportive care; this will be covered in the next glioblastoma lesson           |

Extending maintenance temozolomide beyond six cycles is common in some practices, but randomized evidence has not established an overall-survival advantage. It should be labeled as individualized practice rather than standard evidence-based therapy.

### First post-treatment MRI and pseudoprogression

Obtain the first MRI approximately **3–4 weeks after chemoradiation**, before or around the start of maintenance temozolomide. Under RANO 2.0, this post-radiotherapy MRI becomes the principal baseline for subsequent response assessment.

During the first **12 weeks after radiotherapy**, new or increased enhancement may represent pseudoprogression, especially in an MGMT-methylated tumor. If the patient is clinically stable:

- Do not automatically stop temozolomide.
- Compare steroid dose and neurologic status.
- Review whether enhancement is predominantly within the radiation field.
- Use perfusion MRI, spectroscopy or amino-acid PET selectively.
- Repeat MRI in approximately 4–8 weeks when uncertainty remains.

RANO 2.0 requires radiographic confirmation or unequivocal histopathologic evidence before declaring early progression in many such cases. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37774317/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Afterward, obtain MRI approximately every **2–4 months for the first three years**, then every **3–6 months**, individualized to clinical course.

### Toxicity and supportive care

**During concurrent RT–TMZ**

- Check CBC regularly—commonly weekly during chemoradiation.
- Provide PJP prophylaxis during the 42-day temozolomide regimen and continue if substantial lymphopenia or ongoing immunosuppression persists.
- Give an antiemetic before temozolomide; bedtime dosing often improves tolerability.
- Monitor for thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, lymphopenia, fatigue, nausea, constipation and hepatotoxicity.
- Use dexamethasone only for symptomatic edema and taper to the lowest effective dose.
- Do not routinely prescribe an antiseizure medication to a patient who has never had a seizure.

**With tumor-treating fields**

The principal toxicity is scalp irritation. Preventive scalp care, regular array repositioning and early treatment of dermatitis improve adherence. Clinical benefit is associated with consistent use; patients are generally encouraged to wear the device for at least 18 hours daily.

### Guideline versus evolving practice

- **Guideline standard:** Maximal safe resection followed by RT with concurrent and adjuvant TMZ.
- **Guideline-supported option:** Tumor-treating fields during maintenance therapy.
- **Expert practice:** Obtain a preradiation MRI when postoperative imaging is several weeks old or neurologic status has changed.
- **Uncertain:** Temozolomide beyond six cycles, routine bevacizumab upfront, and routine checkpoint inhibition have not demonstrated a clear overall-survival benefit.
- **Emerging:** Vaccines, cellular therapies, viral therapies, focused ultrasound and molecularly selected targeted treatments remain primarily clinical-trial strategies.

### What I should remember for clinic and boards

- **Fit newly diagnosed GBM:** 60 Gy/30 fractions + daily TMZ, followed by six cycles of TMZ.
- TMZ is given **every day during RT**, including weekends.
- MGMT methylation predicts greater TMZ benefit but does not define glioblastoma.
- Do not routinely omit TMZ solely because MGMT is unmethylated in a fit younger adult.
- Tumor-treating fields are added during maintenance, not during standard chemoradiation.
- The **post-RT MRI is the RANO 2.0 baseline**.
- Increased enhancement within 12 weeks of RT is not automatically progression.
- Upfront bevacizumab is not standard treatment for improving survival.

### Newly diagnosed glioblastoma in an older or frail adult

**Guidance checked October 10, 2026.** NCCN now lists **Central Nervous System Cancers Version 3.2026**. This lesson also incorporates the **2025 ASTRO grade 4 diffuse-glioma guideline** and **2025 SNO–EANO glioblastoma consensus review**. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nccn_nccn-has-recently-published-the-following-activity-7505318003371081728-L4wg?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### The central principle

Do not choose treatment using age alone. “Older” trials commonly used cutoffs of 65–70 years, but the clinically important variables are:

- KPS/ECOG and trajectory after surgery
- Independence in activities of daily living
- Cognition, falls and frailty
- Comorbidities and polypharmacy
- Postoperative neurologic deficits
- Steroid requirement
- MGMT-promoter methylation
- Patient priorities and treatment burden

A brief geriatric screen such as **G8**, followed by a more comprehensive assessment when abnormal, can reveal vulnerability not captured by KPS alone. The 2025 SNO–EANO review emphasizes that frailty and comorbidities may be more useful than chronological age when selecting intensity. [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/neuro-oncology/article/27/11/2751/8237727?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Practical treatment framework

| Clinical setting                                       | Preferred approach                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Fit older adult**, generally KPS ≥70 and independent | Hypofractionated RT **40 Gy/15 fractions over three weeks** with concurrent and adjuvant TMZ                                   |
| Exceptionally fit older adult                          | Standard six-week RT–TMZ remains reasonable, but has not been proven superior to abbreviated chemoradiation in this population |
| Vulnerable patient, **MGMT methylated**                | 40 Gy/15 fractions plus TMZ if tolerable; TMZ alone is reasonable when RT is impractical                                       |
| Vulnerable patient, **MGMT unmethylated**              | Hypofractionated RT alone is generally preferred if combination therapy would be excessive                                     |
| MGMT unknown                                           | Obtain MGMT promptly; if treatment cannot wait, short-course RT is a reasonable backbone                                       |
| Severe frailty or very poor functional status          | Very abbreviated RT, symptom-directed supportive care, or hospice depending on goals and expected burden                       |

The pivotal CE.6 trial enrolled patients aged 65–90 and showed that adding TMZ to 40 Gy/15 fractions improved median survival from **7.6 to 9.3 months**. The benefit was strongest in MGMT-methylated tumors: **13.5 versus 7.7 months**. Quality of life was similar between groups. [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa1611977?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### How to use MGMT

MGMT has greater treatment-selection value in an older or frail patient because you may need to choose only one treatment modality.

- **MGMT methylated:** TMZ is biologically favored. If the patient can tolerate combined treatment, use short-course RT plus TMZ. If only one modality is feasible, TMZ alone is reasonable.
- **MGMT unmethylated:** RT retains activity independent of MGMT. TMZ alone should generally be avoided because benefit is limited.
- **Borderline or “gray-zone” methylation:** Interpret with the assay, tumor cellularity and laboratory cutoff. Avoid treating the result as simply positive or negative when the value is close to threshold.

The Nordic and NOA-08 trials support TMZ selection in methylated tumors and RT selection in unmethylated tumors when single-modality treatment is required. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22877848/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Radiation options

**40 Gy in 15 fractions** is the best-established abbreviated regimen and usually the default for an older or vulnerable adult.

Other regimens include:

- **34 Gy in 10 fractions:** an accepted shorter alternative in selected older patients.
- **25 Gy in 5 fractions:** may be appropriate for marked frailty, major logistical barriers or limited expected survival, although its role is more individualized.
- **60 Gy in 30 fractions:** reasonable for a very fit older patient, but six weeks of treatment should not be assumed superior merely because it is more intensive.

The 2025 ASTRO guideline conditionally recommends hypofractionated RT with concurrent and adjuvant TMZ for older patients and supportive/palliative care for severe frailty after multidisciplinary discussion. [www.astro.org](https://www.astro.org/news-and-publications/news-and-media-center/news-releases/2025/astro-updates-guideline-on-radiation-therapy-for-high-grade-diffuse-glioma?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Temozolomide with short-course RT

During 40 Gy/15 fractions, TMZ is administered at **75 mg/m² daily for the 21-day radiation course**, including weekends, followed by adjuvant TMZ in 28-day cycles if tolerated.

For a vulnerable patient, reassess after chemoradiation rather than committing automatically to prolonged chemotherapy. Consider:

- Recovery of counts and functional status
- Steroid requirement
- Falls, infection or hospitalization
- Radiographic findings
- Whether continuing therapy still matches the patient’s goals

### Follow-up

Obtain the first post-RT MRI approximately **3–4 weeks after treatment**, then generally every **2–4 months**. As with younger patients, worsening enhancement during the first 12 weeks may represent pseudoprogression; do not abandon treatment solely because the first MRI looks worse if the clinical picture remains stable.

In a frail patient, the imaging schedule should serve a clinical purpose. Earlier imaging is appropriate for neurologic decline, rising steroid needs or new seizures; repeated surveillance that would not change management may reasonably be reduced.

### Toxicity and supportive care

Older patients are particularly vulnerable to:

- TMZ-related thrombocytopenia, lymphopenia and infection
- Steroid-induced delirium, hyperglycemia, proximal weakness and falls
- Fatigue, deconditioning and loss of independence
- Medication interactions and adherence problems
- Cognitive decline and caregiver strain

During concurrent RT–TMZ, monitor CBC regularly and provide PJP prophylaxis. Use dexamethasone only for symptomatic edema and taper aggressively but safely. Arrange physical/occupational therapy, fall assessment, diabetes management and home support early.

Early palliative-care involvement is compatible with active treatment. It helps with symptoms, caregiver support, advance-care planning and decisions about whether treatment remains worthwhile.

### Guideline versus individualized practice

- **Guideline-supported standard:** 40 Gy/15 fractions plus concurrent and adjuvant TMZ for a fit older patient.
- **Biomarker-directed guideline practice:** If only one modality is feasible, TMZ for MGMT-methylated disease and RT for unmethylated disease.
- **Expert practice:** Use frailty and function—not an arbitrary birthday—to decide between six-week and three-week chemoradiation.
- **Uncertainty:** Whether very fit older patients benefit more from 60 Gy/30 fractions than 40 Gy/15 fractions with TMZ.
- **Emerging:** A 2024 reanalysis suggests truly unmethylated tumors may derive no meaningful TMZ benefit, but assay cutoffs and “gray-zone” results complicate universal application. [Oxford Academic](https://doi.org/10.1093/neuonc/noae108?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### What I should remember for clinic and boards

- **Age alone does not define frailty.**
- Fit older patient: **40 Gy/15 fractions + concurrent/adjuvant TMZ**.
- If choosing one modality:
  - **MGMT methylated → TMZ**
  - **MGMT unmethylated → RT**
- Do not use TMZ monotherapy for a clearly unmethylated tumor.
- A six-week course is not automatically better for an older patient.
- Supportive care alone is appropriate when treatment burden exceeds likely benefit.
- In older patients, preventing delirium, steroid myopathy and loss of independence is part of oncologic treatment.
