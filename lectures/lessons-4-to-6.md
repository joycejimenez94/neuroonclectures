### First recurrence of glioblastoma: prove progression, then choose salvage therapy

**Guidance checked October 17, 2026**

- [NCCN Central Nervous System Cancers, Version 3.2026](https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/guidelines-detail?category=1\&id=1425)
- [SNO–EANO glioblastoma consensus update, November 2025](https://academic.oup.com/neuro-oncology/article/27/11/2751/8237727?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- [ESTRO–EANO reirradiation guideline, 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167814024043585?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- [RANO 2.0 response criteria, 2023](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.23.01059?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 1. First decide whether this is true progression

New or increased enhancement can reflect recurrent tumor, pseudoprogression, radiation necrosis, postoperative change, ischemia, infection, or seizure-related change.

**Within 12 weeks after radiotherapy:** RANO 2.0 generally requires confirmation by repeat MRI or unequivocal pathology before declaring progression because pseudoprogression is common. If the patient is clinically stable, continue planned therapy and repeat MRI—often in approximately 4–8 weeks. The post-radiotherapy MRI is the response-assessment baseline. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11361798/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Beyond 12 weeks:** a confirmatory scan is not routinely mandatory, but uncertainty remains common. Review:

- Neurologic trajectory, performance status and corticosteroid requirement
- Whether enhancement lies within the prior high-dose radiation field
- T2/FLAIR progression, diffusion and perfusion characteristics
- Treatment timing, especially recent immunotherapy or bevacizumab
- Amino-acid PET or advanced MRI when available—helpful, but not definitive

Resection or biopsy offers the strongest confirmation when tissue acquisition would also relieve symptoms or change management.

### 2. The salvage-treatment decision

There is **no universally accepted survival-prolonging standard at recurrence**. NCCN and SNO–EANO prioritize clinical trials for eligible patients. [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/neuro-oncology/article/27/11/2751/8237727?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

| Clinical situation                                                       | Most useful approach                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| Good performance status and trial available                              | **Clinical trial preferred**        |
| Focal, surgically accessible, symptomatic lesion; low expected morbidity | Repeat resection                    |
| Small/localized recurrence, KPS ≥60, adequate interval from initial RT   | Reirradiation consideration         |
| Diffuse, multifocal or unresectable disease                              | Systemic therapy                    |
| Major edema or steroid dependence requiring rapid relief                 | Bevacizumab-containing strategy     |
| MGMT-methylated tumor with meaningful TMZ-free interval                  | Consider temozolomide rechallenge   |
| Poor performance status or continued rapid decline                       | Symptom-focused care, often hospice |

### 3. Local therapy

**Repeat surgery**

Consider it when maximal or near-maximal removal of enhancing tumor is feasible and the patient has reasonable functional status. The strongest practical indications are mass effect, neurologic symptoms, steroid dependence and need for tissue.

Benefits include decompression, reduction of tumor burden and distinction between viable tumor and treatment effect. However, apparent survival advantages in retrospective series are heavily affected by patient selection; routine “supramaximal” resection at recurrence has no established benefit.

If tissue is obtained, reconfirm the diagnosis and consider updated molecular profiling—particularly when an actionable alteration could open a trial or tumor-agnostic treatment.

**Reirradiation**

The 2025 ESTRO–EANO guideline supports consideration in selected patients with **KPS ≥60** and generally **more than six months since prior radiation**, after multidisciplinary review. Smaller focal targets are the best candidates; stereotactic or hypofractionated approaches are commonly used. [sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167814024043585?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Counsel about radionecrosis, edema, neurologic deterioration and cumulative cognitive injury. Adding systemic treatment to reirradiation has not yet demonstrated a definitive overall-survival advantage.

### 4. Systemic choices

- **Lomustine:** The conventional cytotoxic comparator and a reasonable option for fit patients. Activity is modest—approximately 5–7% response and 6-month progression-free survival of 6–25% in historical trials. Watch for delayed, cumulative thrombocytopenia and neutropenia. [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/neuro-oncology/article/27/11/2751/8237727?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **Bevacizumab:** Useful for edema, steroid reduction, symptom relief and rapid radiographic control. It has not demonstrated an overall-survival benefit. Reduced enhancement may represent vascular normalization—“pseudoresponse”—so judge benefit using function, steroid dose and T2/FLAIR as well as contrast enhancement.
- **Lomustine plus bevacizumab:** In EORTC 26101, the combination improved median PFS from 1.5 to 4.2 months but not OS: 9.1 versus 8.6 months. This is chiefly a disease-control or symptom-directed choice, not a proven survival strategy. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29141164/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **Temozolomide rechallenge:** Most defensible when the tumor is MGMT-promoter methylated, did not progress during TMZ, and the TMZ-free interval is substantial—often at least six months. No alternative dose-dense schedule has shown clear superiority.
- **Molecularly matched therapy:** Consider for uncommon actionable alterations such as BRAF V600E, NTRK fusion or selected FGFR alterations, preferably within a trial and following guideline-concordant molecular testing. [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/neuro-oncology/article/27/2/331/7817138?login=true\&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Important 2026 update:** Regorafenib is no longer an NCCN recurrent-glioblastoma option. GBM AGILE did not confirm an OS or PFS benefit over control and found greater toxicity. [Journal of Clinical Oncology](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO-25-01137?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Follow-up and supportive care

**Expert practice:** obtain an MRI before salvage treatment and approximately every 6–8 weeks initially; extend or individualize the interval according to treatment, clinical stability and goals of care.

- Use the lowest effective dexamethasone dose and taper whenever possible.
- Do not prescribe antiseizure medication prophylactically in a patient who has never seized.
- With bevacizumab, monitor blood pressure, urine protein, thrombosis, bleeding and wound healing; coordinate carefully around surgery.
- With lomustine, monitor CBC because the nadir is delayed; also follow hepatic function and cumulative marrow toxicity.
- Introduce rehabilitation, symptom-focused palliative care and advance-care planning early—not only when tumor-directed therapy stops.

### Guideline versus uncertainty

**Guideline-supported:** clinical-trial preference, individualized selection among surgery, focused reirradiation and systemic therapy; bevacizumab for disease or symptom control; TMZ rechallenge only in selected patients.

**Expert practice:** choosing bevacizumab primarily for steroid-dependent edema or neurologic symptoms rather than enhancement alone, and using short-interval MRI when progression remains uncertain.

**Uncertain or emerging:** survival benefit from repeat surgery or reirradiation, optimal sequencing and combinations, liquid biopsy, vaccines, cellular therapies, oncolytic viruses and other immunotherapies. These remain trial strategies.

### What I should remember for clinic and boards

1. **Do not call early post-radiation enhancement recurrence without confirmation** when the patient is stable.
2. **A clinical trial is the preferred recurrence strategy** for a fit patient.
3. Surgery is most useful for a **focal, symptomatic, safely resectable lesion** and when tissue will matter.
4. Reirradiation fits a **selected, functional patient with limited disease and a sufficient interval from prior RT**.
5. Bevacizumab can improve edema, steroid dependence and PFS—but **not overall survival**.
6. TMZ rechallenge mainly belongs to **MGMT-methylated, TMZ-sensitive disease after a meaningful treatment-free interval**.
7. **Regorafenib has been removed from current NCCN recurrent-GBM guidance.**

### Grade 2 IDH-mutant astrocytoma: surveillance, vorasidenib, or chemoradiation?

**Guidance checked October 24, 2026**

- [NCCN Central Nervous System Cancers, Version 3.2026](https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/guidelines-detail?category=1\&id=1425)
- [ASCO–SNO rapid recommendation update, April 29, 2025](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO-25-00250?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- [ASTRO lower-grade glioma guideline, 2022](https://www.astro.org/provider-resources/guidelines/clinical-practice-guidelines/lower-grade-glioma-guideline?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- [Current FDA vorasidenib prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=31405fee-55b7-4857-987e-2724ee76be84\&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Diagnostic framework

The integrated diagnosis requires:

- An **IDH1 or IDH2 mutation**
- Absence of whole-arm **1p/19q codeletion**
- Grade 2 histology, without necrosis or microvascular proliferation
- No **CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion**, which would establish IDH-mutant astrocytoma, CNS WHO grade 4

ATRX loss and abnormal p53 expression support astrocytic lineage but are not substitutes for appropriate IDH and 1p/19q testing.

### The central treatment question

After maximal safe resection, ask:

> Can definitive radiotherapy and chemotherapy safely be deferred?

| Postoperative setting                                                                                                                                        | Practical approach                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Complete resection, favorable clinical features, no measurable residual disease                                                                              | Active MRI surveillance remains appropriate                             |
| Residual or recurrent measurable, predominantly nonenhancing disease; surgery was the only treatment; no immediate need for RT/chemotherapy                  | **Vorasidenib** is a strong option                                      |
| Progressive, symptomatic, large or infiltrative residual tumor; neurologic deficit, difficult seizures, enhancement or other concern for aggressive behavior | **RT followed by chemotherapy**                                         |
| New enhancement or unexpectedly rapid growth                                                                                                                 | Reconsider sampling/resection to exclude transformation to grade 3 or 4 |

Age ≥40 years and less than gross-total resection defined “high risk” in older trials, but **40 is not a biologic cutoff**. Current decisions should also incorporate residual volume, growth rate, symptoms, location, neurologic function and patient priorities.

### Where vorasidenib fits

The 2025 ASCO–SNO update states that vorasidenib **may be offered when postoperative RT and chemotherapy can safely be deferred**. It should not be used merely to postpone necessary definitive treatment in an aggressive or symptomatic tumor. [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO-25-00250?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The FDA indication includes patients aged 12 or older with susceptible IDH1/2-mutant grade 2 astrocytoma or oligodendroglioma following biopsy or resection. The adult dose is **40 mg orally once daily**, continued until progression or unacceptable toxicity. [fda.gov](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-vorasidenib-grade-2-astrocytoma-or-oligodendroglioma-susceptible-idh1-or-idh2-mutation?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**INDIGO evidence**

INDIGO enrolled patients with:

- Residual or recurrent grade 2 IDH-mutant glioma
- Measurable, predominantly nonenhancing disease
- KPS ≥80
- Surgery as their only previous treatment
- No immediate need for radiotherapy or chemotherapy

Median progression-free survival was **27.7 months with vorasidenib versus 11.1 months with placebo**, and the time to the next intervention was significantly prolonged. [nejm.org](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2304194?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Important limitation: INDIGO did **not** compare vorasidenib with RT plus chemotherapy and excluded patients previously treated with either modality.

### When chemoradiation is preferable

ASCO–SNO strongly recommends **radiotherapy followed by adjuvant chemotherapy with TMZ or PCV** when treatment cannot appropriately be deferred. A typical radiation course is approximately **50.4–54 Gy in 28–30 fractions**. [Journal of Clinical Oncology](https://ascopubs.org/doi/full/10.1200/JCO.21.02036?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The randomized RTOG 9802 trial established the survival benefit of adding six cycles of PCV after RT in historically defined high-risk grade 2 glioma: median OS was **13.3 versus 7.8 years** with RT alone. Subsequent molecular analysis suggested benefit in IDH-mutant subgroups. [NCI](https://www.cancer.gov/news-events/press-releases/2014/rtog9802?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**How to choose the chemotherapy**

- **PCV:** strongest randomized long-term survival evidence, but more myelosuppression, nausea, neuropathy and treatment burden.
- **Temozolomide:** explicitly accepted by ASCO–SNO for grade 2 IDH-mutant astrocytoma and commonly selected for tolerability; its evidence is less direct than the randomized PCV evidence.
- Concurrent TMZ with RT followed by adjuvant TMZ is used in practice, but ASCO–SNO’s formal grade 2 recommendation specifically emphasizes RT with **adjuvant** chemotherapy.

### Surveillance

For grade 2 glioma, NCCN-based follow-up is generally:

- MRI every **3–6 months for five years**
- Then every **6–12 months**, or sooner for symptoms or concerning growth

During active vorasidenib treatment, many centers obtain MRI approximately every three months initially; this is practical expert practice rather than a uniquely proven interval. Slowly enlarging T2/FLAIR disease should be assessed across several scans, ideally with consistent MRI technique. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11467015/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Escalate evaluation for new enhancement, accelerating growth, increasing seizures, cognitive decline or a new neurologic deficit.

### Toxicity and supportive care

**Vorasidenib**

The major treatment-specific concern is hepatotoxicity. Obtain AST, ALT, GGT, alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin:

- Before treatment
- Every **two weeks for the first two months**
- Monthly for the first **two years**
- More frequently if abnormalities develop

Common adverse effects include fatigue, headache, musculoskeletal symptoms, diarrhea and nausea. Tobacco smoking and moderate CYP1A2 inducers can decrease vorasidenib exposure. Review reproductive counseling and drug interactions, including the potential reduction in effectiveness of hormonal contraception. [dailymed.nlm.nih.gov](https://www.dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=31405fee-55b7-4857-987e-2724ee76be84\&type=display\&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**RT plus chemotherapy**

- PCV: delayed and cumulative myelosuppression, nausea, procarbazine interactions and vincristine neuropathy
- TMZ: thrombocytopenia, lymphopenia, nausea, constipation and fatigue
- RT: fatigue, alopecia and potential long-term neurocognitive or endocrine effects depending on the treated volume

Continue active management of seizures, cognition, mood, employment concerns and fertility—particularly because these patients may live for many years.

### Guideline versus uncertainty

**Guideline-supported**

- Surveillance for appropriately selected favorable patients
- Vorasidenib when RT and chemotherapy can safely be deferred
- RT followed by TMZ or PCV when definitive postoperative treatment is required

**Expert practice**

- Interpreting age, residual volume and tumor growth together rather than applying the historical age-40 cutoff mechanically
- Favoring TMZ when PCV toxicity threatens completion or quality of life

**Uncertain**

- Whether vorasidenib ultimately improves overall survival
- Vorasidenib after complete resection without measurable disease
- Its benefit after previous RT or chemotherapy
- Whether initial vorasidenib is superior to immediate chemoradiation for patients suitable for either strategy
- Optimal treatment after progression on an IDH inhibitor

### What I should remember for clinic and boards

1. **IDH-mutant + no 1p/19q codeletion = astrocytoma lineage.**
2. CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion makes the tumor **grade 4**, not grade 2.
3. Vorasidenib is for the setting in which **RT and chemotherapy can safely be deferred**.
4. The best INDIGO match is surgery-only, measurable nonenhancing disease, good performance status and no urgent indication for chemoradiation.
5. Progressive, symptomatic or aggressive disease still requires **RT plus chemotherapy**.
6. PCV has the strongest randomized survival evidence; TMZ is a guideline-accepted, often better-tolerated alternative.
7. With vorasidenib, remember the intensive **liver-test monitoring schedule**.

### Newly diagnosed grade 3 IDH-mutant astrocytoma: what CATNON established

**Guidance checked October 31, 2026**

- [NCCN Central Nervous System Cancers, Version 3.2026 — August 17, 2026](https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/guidelines-detail?category=1\&id=1425) [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nccn_nccn-has-recently-published-the-following-activity-7505318003371081728-L4wg?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- [ASCO–SNO diffuse-glioma guideline update — April 29, 2025](https://www.asco.org/neurooncology-guidelines?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- [ESTRO–EANO radiotherapy guideline — October 2024](https://www.thegreenjournal.com/article/S0167-8140%2824%2903572-2/fulltext?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- [Final CATNON analysis — ](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41449147/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)[*Lancet Oncology*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41449147/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)[, January 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41449147/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Diagnostic framework

The integrated diagnosis requires:

- IDH1 or IDH2 mutation
- No whole-arm 1p/19q codeletion
- Increased mitotic activity supporting grade 3
- No necrosis or microvascular proliferation
- No CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion, which establishes **IDH-mutant astrocytoma, grade 4**

ATRX loss and TP53 alteration commonly support astrocytic lineage. Grade 3 is not defined by contrast enhancement: these tumors may be nonenhancing, while enhancement should prompt careful review for undersampling or transformation.

After maximal safe resection, obtain early postoperative MRI and proceed to adjuvant treatment. Unlike favorable grade 2 disease, routine observation or vorasidenib instead of definitive therapy is not established for grade 3 astrocytoma.

### Standard treatment for a fit adult

The evidence-based regimen is:

1. **Radiotherapy:** 59.4 Gy in 33 fractions.
2. **No concurrent temozolomide is required.**
3. **Adjuvant temozolomide for 12 cycles:**
   - Cycle 1: 150 mg/m² on days 1–5 of a 28-day cycle
   - Later cycles: 200 mg/m² on days 1–5 if tolerated

ASCO–SNO strongly recommends RT followed by adjuvant TMZ. The 2024 ESTRO–EANO guideline recommends 59.4 Gy in 33 fractions for grade 3 IDH-mutant glioma. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12309703/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### The final CATNON result

CATNON used a 2×2 factorial design:

| Treatment component           | Final result                             |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| RT alone                      | Inferior to RT followed by adjuvant TMZ  |
| Concurrent TMZ during RT      | No statistically significant OS benefit  |
| Twelve cycles of adjuvant TMZ | Clear OS benefit                         |
| Concurrent plus adjuvant TMZ  | Benefit was driven by the adjuvant phase |

After a median follow-up of 10.9 years, patients with IDH-mutant tumors receiving adjuvant TMZ had median OS of **12.5 years versus 6.0 years** without adjuvant TMZ; HR 0.54. Concurrent TMZ did not significantly improve OS. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41449147/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The practical conclusion is unusually clean:

> **For grade 3 IDH-mutant astrocytoma, give RT followed by 12 cycles of TMZ. Concurrent TMZ can be omitted.**

NCCN continues to list both RT with adjuvant TMZ—identified as preferred—and RT with concurrent/adjuvant TMZ. The mature CATNON evidence now provides a strong reason to favor the sequential regimen when treating a typical grade 3 tumor.

### Important decision points

| Situation                               | Practical approach                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Gross-total resection                   | Still give RT followed by TMZ; resection does not eliminate the need for adjuvant treatment                   |
| Subtotal resection or biopsy            | Same backbone; consider re-resection only if additional safe cytoreduction is realistically achievable        |
| MGMT unmethylated                       | Do not routinely omit TMZ; MGMT is not used as it is in older glioblastoma                                    |
| KPS below 60 or severe comorbidity      | Individualize with abbreviated RT, TMZ alone in selected cases, or symptom-focused care                       |
| IDH inhibitor requested                 | Not a proven substitute for postoperative RT–TMZ in grade 3 disease                                           |
| CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion discovered | Reclassify as grade 4; management becomes less certain and may follow grade 3 or glioblastoma-type approaches |

CATNON’s exploratory molecular analysis identified poor-prognosis features—including CDKN2A deletion, CDK4 or PDGFRA amplification and greater copy-number alteration—but none reliably predicted absence of benefit from TMZ. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S147020452500614X?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Surveillance

A practical NCCN-based schedule after RT and chemotherapy is:

- Post-RT MRI approximately **3–6 weeks after radiation**
- MRI approximately every **3–4 months for the first five years**
- Thereafter, continue at intervals generally no longer than **six months**, individualized to stability and treatment goals

Shorten the interval for increasing seizures, new neurologic deficits, accelerated T2/FLAIR growth or new enhancement. If an early post-RT MRI is equivocal and the patient remains stable, repeat MRI in approximately 4–8 weeks rather than immediately declaring progression.

### Toxicity and supportive care

**Adjuvant TMZ**

- Check CBC and metabolic/liver testing before each cycle.
- Watch for thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, cumulative lymphopenia, fatigue, nausea and constipation.
- Routine PJP prophylaxis is generally unnecessary with adjuvant-only TMZ unless there is substantial lymphopenia, prolonged corticosteroid use or another immunosuppressive factor.
- Twelve cycles can create cumulative marrow toxicity; hold, reduce or discontinue according to blood counts and clinical tolerance.
- Discuss fertility preservation before RT and prolonged alkylating chemotherapy when relevant.

**Radiotherapy**

Counsel about fatigue, alopecia and delayed neurocognitive effects. Use modern conformal techniques; the ESTRO–EANO guideline recommends inclusion of the resection cavity and residual tumor, with T2/FLAIR-defined infiltrative disease while excluding edema when distinguishable. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39454886/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Continue seizure management, rehabilitation, cognitive assessment and return-to-work support. Avoid chronic dexamethasone unless required for symptomatic edema.

### Guideline versus uncertainty

**Guideline-supported**

- Postoperative RT followed by adjuvant TMZ
- 59.4 Gy in 33 fractions
- Twelve adjuvant TMZ cycles, reflecting CATNON

**Evidence-informed evolution**

- NCCN still permits concurrent/adjuvant TMZ, but final CATNON demonstrates that the survival benefit comes from **adjuvant**, not concurrent, TMZ.

**Uncertain**

- Optimal treatment of IDH-mutant astrocytoma grade 4
- Whether every patient needs all 12 cycles if toxicity develops
- Whether an IDH inhibitor has a role before recurrence in grade 3 disease
- The best salvage sequence at progression

### What I should remember for clinic and boards

1. Grade 3 IDH-mutant astrocytoma is **IDH-mutant and 1p/19q noncodeleted**.
2. CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion makes it **grade 4**.
3. Standard therapy is **59.4 Gy/33 fractions followed by 12 cycles of TMZ**.
4. CATNON showed a major survival benefit from **adjuvant TMZ**.
5. CATNON did **not** show a significant benefit from concurrent TMZ.
6. Gross-total resection does not justify observation in grade 3 disease.
7. Vorasidenib is not an established replacement for chemoradiation in this setting.
